**Central question.** How does the *past* of training load and wellness shape current risk—and how should that memory enter statistical models of injury and recurrent events?

**Methods.** Time-varying exposure models; piecewise / additive hazard frameworks; weighted cumulative exposure and related lag structures; latent-state (HMM) views of wellness–load dynamics; competing risks and event history.

**Why it matters.** Athlete monitoring systems collect dense longitudinal signals, but decisions still often rely on crude ratios or short windows. The scientific goal is to make **temporal memory** an explicit, estimable object—not an afterthought.

**Selected evidence.** Flexible modelling of time-varying exposures and recurrent events; sports-injury prediction with recurrent time-to-event models; software and tooling around injury analytics (`injurytools` line of work).
