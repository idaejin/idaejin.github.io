**Central question.** In longitudinal and nonlinear mixed-effects models, *what drives predictive uncertainty over time*—and can we attribute that uncertainty in a scientifically interpretable way?

**Methods.** Time-varying Sobol / global sensitivity indices; bootstrap and simultaneous inference bands; connections to GAM / mixed-model prediction; mixed-frequency and forecasting cousins where temporal resolution itself is part of the problem.

**Why it matters.** Prediction intervals alone do not say *why* uncertainty rises near puberty, after a sparse observation window, or at a forecast horizon. Attribution turns uncertainty from a diagnostic into a research object.

**Selected evidence.** Ongoing Dynamic Sobol programme for longitudinal NLME / related models; forecasting and mixed-frequency modelling threads where uncertainty and temporal aggregation interact.
