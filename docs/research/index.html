    Overview

    I work on flexible statistical modelling—P-splines, mixed models, and survival / PAMM—for longitudinal and event-history data in health and sports science. The contributions below are the public face of that programme; the trajectory section sketches how the path was built.

    → People · Projects · Publications

    Main contributions

    P-splines, mixed models, and survival (PAMM)

    The methodological core of my work is flexible regression via penalized splines (P-splines), their mixed-model representation, and survival / event-history methods—including piecewise exponential additive mixed models (PAMM) for time-to-event and recurrent-event data with smooth effects and time-varying covariates.

    This toolkit covers multidimensional and adaptive smoothers, ANOVA-type interactions, GLAM-type computation, and hazard models where exposure histories and nonlinear effects enter additively.

    Selected anchors: multidimensional adaptive P-splines; out-of-sample prediction in multidimensional P-spline models; fast smoothing-parameter separation in multidimensional GLAM settings; flexible modelling of time-varying exposures and recurrent events (PAMM).

    → Publications · S3M1P4R · AFTERAM · SPHERES

    Growth curves, derivatives, and maturation

    A second line focuses on longitudinal growth: curve and derivative estimation, maturation timing (including peak height velocity / APHV), and shape-constrained approaches that keep models usable for reference-to-individual prediction in youth sport and paediatric settings.

    Selected anchors: derivative-curve estimation with P-splines; Bayesian hierarchical modelling of growth-curve derivatives; peak height velocity and injury burden in youth football; SPHERES.

    → Publications · MATH4SPORTS

    Sports injury and recurrent-event modelling

    With collaborators in sports medicine and statistics, I work on injury risk as a recurrent-event problem: time-varying training exposures, PAMM / additive hazard models, and tools that make longitudinal athlete-monitoring data analysable.

    Selected anchors: flexible modelling of time-varying exposures and recurrent events; prediction of sports injuries in football with recurrent time-to-event models; the injurytools software line; MATH4SPORTS.

    → Publications · People

    Patient-reported outcomes and joint survival

    A long-running health line develops beta-binomial and related models for longitudinal patient-reported outcomes (PROs), including joint modelling with survival endpoints (e.g. COPD applications).

    Selected anchors: beta-binomial mixed-effects models for longitudinal PROs; multidimensional beta-binomial regression; joint PRO–survival analysis in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

    Trajectory

    Universidad Carlos III de Madrid (to 2011). Training and early research in statistics and mathematical engineering; PhD in Statistics (2010). First academic appointments as teaching assistant and lecturer.

    CSIRO / Data61, Melbourne (2011–2014). Postdoctoral Fellow in mathematical and statistical sciences, with a strong applied-modelling orientation.

    BCAM – Basque Center for Applied Mathematics (2014–2022). Led the Applied Statistics research line and, from 2020, coordinated Data Science and Artificial Intelligence in the Knowledge Transfer Unit—bridging academic methods with industry and public-sector problems (including SME advisory work and the Basque Modelling Task Force during COVID-19).

    IE University (2023–). Tenure-track Assistant Professor in Statistical Sciences at the School of Science & Technology, continuing methods and applications in health and sports science, doctoral supervision, and competitive research programmes such as SPHERES.
