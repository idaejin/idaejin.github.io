    Research identity

    My work sits at the intersection of biostatistics and sports science. The methodological signature is flexible regression—P-splines, mixed models, survival analysis—and the scientific through-line is temporal structure: how trajectories, past exposures, and uncertainty evolve.

    Rather than a flat list of papers, the programme is organised around four themes. Publications and funded projects are evidence for these themes; they are not the identity itself.

    Growth, maturation & athletic ageing

    Central question. How can shape-constrained and nonlinear mixed models support reference-to-individual growth prediction, maturation timing (APHV), and athletic ageing trajectories?

    Methods. I/M-splines and monotone P-splines; SITAR-type growth models; derivative estimation; hierarchical / mixed-effects frameworks.

    Why it matters. In youth sport and paediatric health, decisions depend on where someone is on their developmental curve—not only on calendar age. In adult athletes, the same trajectory language helps describe decline, recovery, and long-term capacity.

    Selected evidence. Work on peak height velocity and injury burden in youth football; derivative-curve estimation in longitudinal studies; ongoing methods for shape-constrained growth curves and athletic-ageing envelopes.

    → Related publications · funded work under SPHERES and MATH4SPORTS

    Load, injury risk & temporal memory

    Central question. How does the past of training load and wellness shape current risk—and how should that memory enter statistical models of injury and recurrent events?

    Methods. Time-varying exposure models; piecewise / additive hazard frameworks; weighted cumulative exposure and related lag structures; latent-state (HMM) views of wellness–load dynamics; competing risks and event history.

    Why it matters. Athlete monitoring systems collect dense longitudinal signals, but decisions still often rely on crude ratios or short windows. The scientific goal is to make temporal memory an explicit, estimable object—not an afterthought.

    Selected evidence. Flexible modelling of time-varying exposures and recurrent events; sports-injury prediction with recurrent time-to-event models; software and tooling around injury analytics (injurytools line of work).

    → Related publications · Projects

    Dynamic uncertainty attribution

    Central question. In longitudinal and nonlinear mixed-effects models, what drives predictive uncertainty over time—and can we attribute that uncertainty in a scientifically interpretable way?

    Methods. Time-varying Sobol / global sensitivity indices; bootstrap and simultaneous inference bands; connections to GAM / mixed-model prediction; mixed-frequency and forecasting cousins where temporal resolution itself is part of the problem.

    Why it matters. Prediction intervals alone do not say why uncertainty rises near puberty, after a sparse observation window, or at a forecast horizon. Attribution turns uncertainty from a diagnostic into a research object.

    Selected evidence. Ongoing Dynamic Sobol programme for longitudinal NLME / related models; forecasting and mixed-frequency modelling threads where uncertainty and temporal aggregation interact.

    → Related publications

    Methods & software engines

    Central question. How do we make flexible longitudinal methods computationally and scientifically reusable—across PRO/survival problems, multidimensional smoothing, and applied pipelines?

    Methods. Multidimensional and adaptive P-splines; beta-binomial and related models for patient-reported outcomes; joint modelling of longitudinal PRO and survival; TMB / Template Model Builder implementations; open software for applied workflows.

    Why it matters. Themes 1–3 share a methodological core. Packaging that core as engines and tools is what lets the same ideas travel from youth growth curves to COPD patient-reported outcomes to athlete monitoring.

    Selected evidence. Multidimensional adaptive P-splines; beta-binomial mixed models for longitudinal PROs; joint PRO–survival analysis in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; knowledge-transfer and software-oriented projects.
